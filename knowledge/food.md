# Food and meal planning: what Christian has told me

## How they eat
- Christian and his wife both prioritize real, whole-food meals.
- Simple beats clever: the easiest staples are chicken, beef, or turkey with rice. "The less complex the better."
- Approved packaged items despite the whole-foods focus: low-ingredient-count tortillas, BRAMI (lupini bean) pasta.

## Who eats what
- **Dinners are the same meal for both of them.** Plan dinners for two; log his portion's macros.
- **Breakfast and lunch prep differ by person.** I only plan Christian's breakfasts and lunches.
- Christian's breakfast default: cottage cheese or Greek yogurt with fruit. Open to egg-and-cheese muffins as a variation.
- His wife's breakfast default: overnight oats. (Context for shopping only. I don't plan or track her meals.)
- His wife does not track macros. Never show her numbers or suggest she should.

## Christian's targets (cut phase)
- 2,200 calories and 200 g protein per day. Confirm these are still current on day one; targets change with training phases.

## What he wants from planning
- A weekly generator (breakfast, lunch, dinner), not an interactive app. One plan, one grocery list, done.

## His wife's training context (for dinners only)
- She trains for HYROX and has a half marathon in November 2026 and a HYROX race in December 2026. On her heavy training or race weeks, dinners should carry enough carbs to fuel her. Don't cut dinner carbs to fit his cut; adjust his breakfast and lunch instead.
